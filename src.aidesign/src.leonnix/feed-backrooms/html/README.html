FEED: the player is the monster

FEED is a first-person horror game in a Backrooms building. The inversion is the whole design. The player is not a person who runs from a monster. The player is the monster. Eight people enter the building. Most carry torches. The player must catch all eight before hunger reaches zero. The source states this in index.html and in the game state at src/main.js:65-69. The title screen says "they wandered in. you were already here."

The inversion changes every system. The lighting is not only a look. The baked light value also sets how far a person can see the monster. The people are not only targets. Their fear, their screams and their panic spread are the feedback that tells the player the hunt is working. The monster's three senses are not a menu. Sight is the camera. Hearing is the sound mix. Smell is a rendering mode that draws body heat and scent trails through walls. Hunger is the only clock. No person can kill the player. The run ends when hunger reaches zero, or three seconds after the eighth person falls.

This package reads the source at Git pin 81cdb6a. It does not run the game. It does not build the bundle. It does not open a browser. It does not play the trailer or listen to the audio. Every claim below comes from the source, the manifests or the project README. Where the source gives no value, this package says so.

The play loop in one minute

The player starts at the centre of macro cell (6, 6). The four movement keys W, A, S and D move the monster. The mouse turns the view. A held shift key runs. Running drains stamina at 0.16 per second, and walking restores it at 0.12 per second. Exhaustion starts at zero stamina and ends at 0.3 stamina. Smelling multiplies the move speed by 0.42, so the player must choose between speed and information.

A left click winds up a claw strike. The strike lands after 0.42 of a 0.5 second animation and adds a forward lunge of 8 metres per second. The cooldown is 0.72 seconds. A roar has an 11 second cooldown. It freezes every person within 14 metres. Beyond 14 metres, only a person who is wandering becomes alert to the player's position. The roar also raises the camera field of view (FOV) by 8 degrees for 0.8 seconds and adds a shake.

Hunger starts at 0.8. It falls at 1/150 per second, so it reaches zero after 120 seconds from the start. Each kill adds 0.36 and is capped at 1. A warning message appears below 0.25. The run ends in one of two ways. Hunger reaches zero, or all eight people fall. The source does not give the player a second life and does not give the people a way to hurt the player.

The building and its eight people

The building is one square of 98 metres. The level grid is 49 cells each side, and each cell is 2 metres. The 49 value is 12 macro cells times 4 cells, plus one closing row and column. A 12 by 12 macro graph sets the room walls. Each interior partition is open, a wall, or a wall with a doorway. A union-find pass tracks groups of macro cells. It reopens one closed wall for each merge that joins two separate groups, so every room is reachable. A sealed pair is two neighbouring macro cells whose shared edge is closed and whose groups differ. The source states this guarantee in src/level.js:148-153. The generator is the subject of 02, so this file gives only the shape.

One ceiling panel sits over each macro cell, so the level has 144 panels. A panel is dead, steady or flickering. The dead probability rises with a separate low-frequency dark field. A field is a 5 by 5 lattice of random values with smooth interpolation. The generator builds a second 5 by 5 lattice, the density field, which sets each wall probability. Each panel feeds the baked light grid. A small pool of real spotlights follows the nearest working panels, so the shader light count never changes.

Eight people enter the building. Each person uses shared low-poly shapes, which are shapes with few faces. The shapes are a five-sided limb cylinder, a six-sided torso, an icosahedron head (a 20-sided solid), boxes, a tube and an open cone for the torch beam. Each person gets a skin, shirt, pants and hair colour from short palettes. Most people carry a torch in the right hand. Every person has a thermal ghost copy (a second copy of each body mesh drawn hot and through walls), a beating heart and a body-heat halo for the smell mode. A state machine is the named state and the rules that move a person between states. Fear shows on the face: the eyes grow, the pupils shrink, the brows tilt and the mouth opens. Fear also adds a tremor to the limbs.

The three senses

Sight is the camera and the people's vision. The player sees the world through a warped lens with grain and colour fringing. Each person sees the monster only if three tests pass. The person needs a grid line of sight (LOS) to the monster. The monster must lie inside the view cone. The cone half-angle is 1.3 radians on each side of the person's facing direction. The monster must also lie inside a range. The range is 6.5 metres plus 15 metres times a light factor from 0 to 1. A lit monster is visible from up to 21.5 metres. A monster in the dark is visible from 6.5 metres. A working torch raises the range to at least 15 metres when the monster is within 0.42 radians of the person's facing direction. Fleeing and cowering people add 6 metres, but the LOS test stops at 26 metres. The effective maximum is therefore 26 metres for those people. Within 3 metres, the cone test is skipped, but LOS is still required. The dark therefore hides the player, and the level light grid is a gameplay value.

Smell is a rendering mode. The world dims to 1 - 0.86 * sense. The value sense is a smoothed number from 0 to 1 that rises while the player holds the smell control. The light grid intensity, the panel colour and the post-process colour all follow the same value. Thermal ghosts, hearts, halos and scent trails draw with depth testing off. The depth test normally hides a pixel behind a nearer surface. With the test off, a wall does not hide these layers. The player can track a person through a wall. Scent trails keep 44 points per person (src/main.js:152), live 32 seconds (:168) and appear only above sense 0.02 (:212).

Hearing is two systems. The game plays positional sounds for people's steps and screams. It plays other sounds for the monster, the interface and the looped heartbeat. The people also have a hearing test. A person hears the monster at 2.4 metres. A person hears a running monster at 9.5 metres. A scream spreads panic to other people within 15 metres. A scream drives both the sound mix and the panic spread from one function.

Why the package is worth reading

The project is small, but it joins several mechanisms without a game engine. One material patch serves walls, floors, people and claws. Two shader variants cover every surface, and the differences are uniforms. One baked light grid serves both the visual light and the gameplay visibility. The game holds one monster breadth-first search (BFS) field for fleeing. Each person holds a separate BFS field for its wander target. A fixed pool of lights moves between fixtures, so the shader program count stays fixed. The trailer recorder films the real game one tick at a time and mixes the real sound calls after the take. The deep dives show how each mechanism works and where it stops.

Non-claims

This package is a static reading. It does not run the game, the development server, the production build, the trailer recorder or the audio mixer. It does not open the trailer file or the three still images. It does not use the network. It does not install a dependency.

No frame was rendered for this package. The visual descriptions come from the shader source, the material source and the project README. The project README calls the player controls and the visual effects, and this package quotes that text as the project's own statement. It does not verify the look.

No sound was played. The audio description comes from src/audio.js and public/assets/sfx/manifest.json. The trailer mix is read from tools/record.js. The loudness, the pan and the final waveform were not measured.

The performance values are code values. The render budget, the pixel-ratio cap, the frame-time threshold and the scale-down factor come from src/main.js. The source does not include a benchmark result. No script in the checkout measures frame rate. The section "The limits of the game" in document 04 states this limit.

The checkout has no licence file for the code. The project README gives the asset licences only. The textures are Creative Commons Zero (CC0) and the sounds are Creative Commons Zero (CC0) or Mixkit Sound Effects Free License. This package does not give legal advice and does not verify those licences.

The determinism claim is narrow. One seed drives the level and the people. A take is deterministic in motion only while the order of random calls stays fixed. Several presentation systems call Math.random directly: the sound pick and rate, the blood sparks and decal, the flicker interval, the scent trail height and the message choice. Those details differ between takes. Document 04 gives the lines.
