04 Limits and comparison: what this method buys, and what it cannot prove

This document records the limits of the afterglow package and compares the method with other ways to make a film. It separates three kinds of statement. A source fact is in the checkout. A project claim comes from the project documents and was not measured here. A judgement is this exploration's reading. 01-architecture.md gives the layers, 02-core-mechanism.md gives the frame math, 03-workflow-and-quality.md gives the gates, and 00-overview.md gives the film and the numbers. The index is README.md.

1. The artifact boundary

The checkout is not the film. The repository holds the source, the score data, the pianist asset and the documents. The generated files are excluded. Those files are the audio, the performance tracks, the FFmpeg Video Codec 1 (FFV1) chunks, the encoded films and the snapshots. A snapshot is a frozen copy of the input directories, not a still image. The film is a release download.

# README.md:261-262
Not in the repository: `build/` (everything generated: audio, tracks, chunks, films, snapshots), the reference images
(`refs/`, licence unknown), the downloaded samples (`music/samples/`), `node_modules/` and `.venv/`.

Two assets are large. The pianist binary glTF (GLB) and the Detmold impulse response are Git Large File Storage (LFS) files. A plain clone can give a text pointer instead of the 24-megabyte GLB. The v1.0 GitHub release holds both files, and the release notes hold their Secure Hash Algorithm 256 bit (SHA-256) hashes. The piano sample set is not in the repository either. It is 1.35 gibibytes and needs a separate download.

The performance tracks are a release file. The project states that the solver code changed after the released run. A fresh solve is therefore a different performance, not the film's performance. The source does not itemise the solver change. At the pin, the released performance and the solver version are fixed constants of the film. The quoted command reports quality control (QC) time.

Note identifiers are zero-based, so the last note identifier is 1130 and the last release belongs to identifier 1129. The score file, in JavaScript Object Notation (JSON), also has "lead_in": 0.0. That is the score's own time origin, not the film's 4.0-second music offset. The film is longer than the duration field, because the edit ends after the final pedal release. A change to the composition invalidates the performance, the hand track and the motion-boost table. The camera cuts are anchored to bars and beats, so a tempo change also moves every cut.

Aha: the render budget is a look decision that is written as a performance decision. N sets the sample count, but it also sets the gap-fill sigma, the motion-copy spacing and the noise floor, which is the base level of sampling noise. The project could not lower N for free. It paired each cut with a test or a boost table. A reader who copies only the N values, without the pre-blur formula and the boost table, gets a different look.

3. Limits the project states

The project is direct about its weakest images. Shot S14 is the only top-down shot, and it lasts 7.3 seconds. Free fingers are fingers that hover and do not touch a key. They splay and curl, and the water near the bench shows hard black blotches.

# README.md:268-270
- **S14, the one top-down shot (7.3 s), is the weakest.** From directly above, the hovering free fingers of both hands
  splay and curl and the left thumb points across at the right hand; the water around the bench shows hard-edged
  black blotches.

The hands are the main limit. Thumbs often stay stiff and stick out. Nine one-frame finger flicks above 12 degrees remain in readable shots. Fast right-hand leaps in S04 travel about 100 pixels per frame, but the motion blur is smooth. The nails in the S06 macro read as dark red polish (README.md:271-274).

The world has limits. The far mountains are a uniform row with little layering. Some ranges read as smooth pyramids. The clouds are smaller than the reference clouds. The water in the wides is more mauve than warm orange. Shot S20 has a foil-like micro-pattern. Shot S25 has white keys that barely read. Lacquer edges show thin dotted highlights that change from frame to frame, because there is no specular anti-aliasing (README.md:276-281). Specular anti-aliasing filters highlights against normal and roughness variation.

The encode has a visible limit. The 8-bit H.264 (Advanced Video Coding) shows faint one-code-value bands in smooth sunset skies (README.md:282). A 10-bit encode would remove them. The project also notes that the shirt cuffs are uniform and the S11 shoe is featureless. The probe samples 30 notes. The README says six of 30 probed hammers peak a fraction of a degree short of the string, so 24 of 30 reach it (README.md:285). An integration document says seven of 30, so 23 of 30 (docs/INTEGRATION.md:128). Six of 30 is the final count; seven of 30 is an older record. The source does not reconcile them. QC measures the written tracks at 60 hertz, so it cannot see a peak between samples; that limit can explain the difference.

The reproducibility claim is scoped. The project tested one Windows machine and one GPU. Other GPUs and drivers give slightly different pixels. The quotation below lists the lookup table (LUT) fitter.

# README.md:290-293
- **Reproducibility**: tested on one Windows machine and GPU; other GPUs and drivers will give slightly different
  pixels. The reference images are not included. Several development tools (look-dev harnesses, the critics' check
  scripts, the LUT fitter, the shot-list generator) lived in the local working area and are not part of the release;
  the files they produced are committed.

The reference images are not included, and refs/ has no known licence. A reader cannot repeat the visual comparison that drove the look. The development tools that produced the comparison are outside the release; the files that those tools produced are committed.

4. Limits inside the mechanism

Four limits are inside the mechanism. The first is motion-blur stepping. At N of 32, a fast hand in an 85-millimetre close-up shows separate copies at its edges. The boost table raises N on the affected frames. The project states that a velocity gap-fill pass is not implemented. It is the analogue of the depth-of-field pre-blur, and it would need per-sub-frame motion vectors of the skinned hands. The next quotation names the depth-of-field (DOF) pre-blur.

# docs/RENDERING.md:449-455
- **Motion-blur stepping.** At N=32, a fast hand in an 85 mm close-up shows discrete copies at its edges. Since fix
  round 4 the motion N boost (section 4, `N`) raises N on exactly those frames: the table is solved from the perf FK
  fingertips/knuckles projected through the edit camera (`sandbox/integration/blurstep.mjs --boost-out`, target copy
  spacing 1 px), re-solved for every snapshot by `render/snapshot.mjs`, and keyed to the perf sha and each shot's N
  (a stale table is ignored by the page and fails integration wiring check 9). A velocity gap-fill pass (the
  analogue of the DOF pre-blur) is still not implemented: it would need per-sub-frame motion vectors of the skinned
  hands.

The second is the depth-of-field pre-blur. It uses the water surface depth, not the reflected path length. A reflected path is longer than the surface depth, so its circle of confusion is sized too small. Reflections in very shallow focus are under-filled. The lens sampling is still correct. The error is in the depth used to size the blur, not in the aperture pattern.

# docs/RENDERING.md:456-457
- **The DOF pre-blur uses the water surface depth**, not the reflected path length, so it under-fills reflections in
  very shallow focus. The lens sampling itself is still correct (research/rendering.md section 4).

The third limit is in the hash coverage. The settings hash covers the image size, the init options and the frame options. It is a fast skip test, not a provenance record. It does not cover the code. The manifest covers every file that the page loaded for that chunk. A file that the page never requested is not covered. A lazy asset that one chunk did not need is outside the gate.

# docs/RENDERING.md:458-462
- **Chunk validity and code changes.** The settings hash covers the size, the init options and the frame options,
  but not the code. The manifests record the content hash of every file the page loaded (code, shots.js, data such as
  `score.json` and `perf.bin`), render.mjs reports stale chunks, `--rerender-stale` re-renders them, and encode.mjs
  refuses to join chunks whose inputs differ. Files the page never requested (e.g. a module's lazily fetched asset
  that a given chunk did not need) are not covered.

The fourth limit is sampling at low N. The sub-frame sample table uses a two-dimensional correlated multi-jittered (CMJ) pattern with one point per cell. It uses a deterministic local search for the pairing of points to sub-frames (scene/src/core/rng.js:61-76; see 02-core-mechanism.md section 8). Four points cannot decorrelate every dimension. The project gives a worst-case defocus-versus-motion asymmetry of 13 percent at N of 4. The source gives no definition, and this exploration cannot settle it. It also gives a ratio of 1.03 on its pan test, which is about 3 percent above one. The time-to-aperture correlation is 0.08 and 0.12; the source gives the two values without labels (docs/RENDERING.md:464-466). These measures use different scales, so they are not directly comparable. Final renders use N of 16 or more. A preview at N of 4 is for wiring, not for a look decision.

5. Limits in the performance

The performance is solved, not captured. The solution has measured errors. The last full run in the performance document reports 382 joint-limit violations, all in the wrist; the source gives the count, not the magnitude of each violation. It reports 108 keyframes with an unreachable contact. An unreachable contact is a fingertip that cannot reach the key plane within the reach limit. A keyframe is one solved palm pose per onset group (docs/PERFORMANCE.md:94). It reports 53,615 white-key and 78,936 black-key penetration samples above one millimetre. Penetration samples are skinned-vertex by frame samples, not audio samples. It reports 6,337 finger pops above 12 degrees per film frame (docs/PERFORMANCE.md:231, 236-240). A finger pop is a one-frame angular jump above 12 degrees. The README counts visible one-frame flicks in readable hand shots; this count sums every finger and film frame. It reports contact error at key bottom (defined in 03-workflow-and-quality.md section 4). The 50th percentile is 0.050 millimetres, the 99th percentile is 0.309 millimetres and the maximum is 3.06 millimetres. During hold, the 99th percentile is 0.31 millimetres and the maximum is 6.53 millimetres (docs/PERFORMANCE.md:227-228). The 0.309 and 0.31 values are different metrics, at key bottom and during hold. It reports 122 early key releases not covered by the pedal, with a median rise of 84 milliseconds before the designed release point (docs/PERFORMANCE.md:238). These are historical measurements from the project's last full run, run C. Run C is the third full solver run of that session and the shipped one (docs/PERFORMANCE.md:221-223). This exploration did not measure them. They show the kind of residual a solver leaves.

6. Limits in the documents

The documents do not always agree. Two disagreements and one agreement follow.

First, the default sun arc in the constants file starts at +0.6 degrees. The edit starts at -0.8 degrees and ends at -4.5 degrees. Both arcs end at -4.5 degrees; the edit overrides the default, so the film uses the edit arc. A reader who imports DEFAULT_SUN_KEYS without the edit gets the old arc. The project statement follows the edit.

Second, the two final-freeze lists agree: nine shots at 48 to 32 and one shot at 64 to 32 (docs/INTEGRATION.md:893-895, scene/src/camera/shots.js:36-37). This document uses that count. The two lists are the integration table and the edit comment.

Third, the performance document says 13,888 frames. The film is 13,857 frames. The performance document describes a run against a score revision from 2026-10-21. The film length comes from the edit, not from the score's duration field. The score ends at 220.7512 seconds. The final pedal pair is [221.4592, 0], where 0 is the pedal fully up (music/final/score.json:18035). The end anchor is pedalUp+5.5s: add 5.5 seconds for the reverb tail and fade to get 226.9592 seconds. Add the 4.0-second film lead-in to get 230.9592 seconds. The frame count is the floor of that time times 60, which is 13,857 frames (scene/src/camera/score.js:182-183). The frame grid ends at 230.95 seconds, the last frame starts at 230.9333 seconds, and the edit end is 230.9592 seconds. The older performance run is historical, not a second film.

The soundtrack has a related mismatch. The score ends at 220.7512 seconds. The soundtrack is 227.5 seconds, so it has about 6.75 seconds of tail. The film places it after a 4.0-second lead-in, so the audio ends at 231.5 seconds. The film ends at 230.95 seconds on the frame grid. The encoder trims the audio to that grid, so the last 0.55 seconds of tail are cut. The tail is reverb, not a lost note. The project does not state a different rule.

8. Comparison with other methods

The table compares the method with four other ways to make a moving image. The comparison is about the properties that this project needs: deterministic output, true lens and shutter sampling, a data-driven edit, and a cost that one person can pay. It is not a claim that one method is better in general.

The two methods closest to this project are the path tracer and the real-time engine. A path tracer solves light transport and traces reflections, refraction and shadows. It also gives DOF and motion blur, but at a high sample cost. The source gives no path-tracer cost measurement, so this comparison is qualitative. The afterglow renderer does not solve global illumination per sub-frame. It uses a sky lookup table and a per-frame sky and cloud cube bake: the sky is rendered into six cube faces. It captures a prefiltered mipmapped radiance environment map (PMREM) for image-based lighting: the cube map is blurred for rough reflections. It renders the lake with a planar reflector, which draws the scene as a mirrored copy (scene/src/world/index.js:7-8, 105-114, 336-345). That choice makes 13,857 frames possible on one laptop. It also limits the image: the world is a designed set, not a general light simulation.

A real-time engine can also render 60 fps, and it can do it interactively. The afterglow method gives up interactivity for sample quality and determinism. It renders one frame at a time, with no frame-to-frame feedback except the small scene history. Every frame can take seconds. That is too slow for a game and fast enough for a film.

Live action gives a real lens, a real shutter and a real performance. It does not give a deterministic rebuild. It cannot change the sun arc by a few degrees and re-render the film. It also needs a lake, a grand piano, a pianist and a sunset. The afterglow method trades physical production for data and compute.

Generative video is the newest alternative. It can make plausible frames from text or images. It does not give a frame-exact performance, a hashed lossless master or a split licence that a viewer can inspect. This exploration makes no claim about its quality. The comparison is about provenance and control, not about appearance.

9. What the gates do not prove

The gates are consistency checks. The self test proves its checks. It checks accumulation alpha at every pixel and glare energy conservation. It checks an identity lookup table (LUT), which changes no pixel by more than one code value. It checks that a lost context throws instead of returning a stale frame. The determinism check proves that three sessions agree. The chunk manifests prove that a chunk came from the recorded settings and inputs. The encode checks prove that the output frames match the rendered frames and that the soundtrack sits at the recorded offset.

They do not prove that the film is good. They do not prove that the performance is musical. They do not prove that the sky matches the reference. They do not prove that the grade is correct on a calibrated display. They cannot see a wrong thumb pose that is deterministic, hashed and bit-identical. A gate can only test the property that it measures.

Aha: every quality gate in this project tests consistency, not truth. The strongest claim the pipeline can make is that the delivered frames are the frames that the source, the settings and the performance define. It cannot make the further claim that those frames are the right frames. That second claim needs a human review. The project records that review in docs/INTEGRATION.md, which names a sandbox/integration/clipreview.py script that the source at ced1038 does not contain.

10. When this method fits

The method fits a project with these properties. The image is planned, not captured. The camera and the light can be data. The performance can be solved or keyed. The output must be repeatable. The budget is one machine and a long overnight run. The film can trade interactive speed for sample quality. The licence must separate code from composition.

The method does not fit a project with these properties. The output must be interactive. The scene needs general global illumination that a lighting probe cannot approximate. The performance must be captured from a real person. The schedule cannot wait for a 21.8-hour render. The team cannot freeze the score and the scene for the length of the run. The licence must be a single open licence for every file.

11. Where to go next

The limits are not defects in the reading. They are the price of the design. The renderer buys determinism and true lens sampling with sample cost and a narrow hardware claim. The performance solver buys control with residual contact error. The licence buys study with a reserved film. The gates buy provenance with a blind spot for taste. 03-workflow-and-quality.md gives the gates in order. 02-core-mechanism.md gives the mechanism behind them. 01-architecture.md gives the layers, and 00-overview.md gives the film and the numbers.
