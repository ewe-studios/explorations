Golden Temple means a Japanese temple complex at the golden hour. The package builds the complex in code. It loads no three-dimensional model, texture file, high dynamic range image (HDRI) or photograph at runtime. JavaScript creates the buildings, roof tiles, bracket sets, gold fittings, statues, trees, lotus, koi, water, terrain, clouds and sky. Graphics Library Shading Language (GLSL) code supplies every surface detail. A few textures are drawn onto canvases at load time.

The world uses metres. Positive X is east, positive Y is up and positive Z is south, so north is negative Z. The courtyard ground is at Y equals zero. The site plan holds the hall at (10, 0, -22), the pagoda at (-32, 0, -8), the gate at (10, 0, 46) and the pond outline. The sun stands 6.8 degrees above the horizon in the west-south-west.

The film has ten shots of seven seconds each, for 70 seconds in total. Each shot opens on one detailed object and moves back to show the complex around it. Every frame is a set of sub-frames. Each sub-frame has a different camera position, sun position and shutter time. The average gives anti-aliasing, motion blur, soft sun shadows and clean ambient occlusion.

2. Scope

This package covers Golden Temple as a system. It covers these areas:

  • The visual idea: a low sun, a warm stone court, vermilion lacquer, gold leaf and a mirror pond.
  • The engine: sky, aerial perspective, ambient volumes, image-based light, shadow fitting and the

post pipeline.

  • The materials: the patch() hook system and the shared procedural materials.
  • The world modules: nine build(ctx) modules, their contracts, their helpers and their data

exchange.

  • The shots: ten camera definitions, camera overrides, easing, smoothing and focus.
  • The frame: sub-frame sampling, the stability rule, anti-aliasing, motion blur, shadows and

ambient occlusion.

  • The film path: headless Chrome, raw pixel transfer, ffmpeg, the music edit and the splice tool.
  • The quality tools: camera motion, brightness flicker, motion-compensated shimmer and contact

sheets.

  • The limits: absent assets, runtime-only values, stale comments and tool assumptions.

It does not run Golden Temple. It does not start Vite, open a browser, build the world, compile a shader, render a frame, encode a film, fetch music or measure a metric. All runtime numbers come from the project documents and are marked as not verified.

4. Constraints

  • The source is read only. No golden-temple file is modified.
  • No server, browser, still capture, world build, shader compile, frame render, film encode or

music download is run.

  • No network request is made. No dependency is installed.
  • Prose uses Aerospace and Defence Simplified Technical English, issue 100 (ASD-STE100). One idea

per sentence. Thirty words maximum. Expand every abbreviation at first use in each file.

  • Prose avoids marketing language, em dashes, "simply", "just", and "obviously".
  • A value that the source does not define is recorded as not defined.
  • The pin is 97fd9ec. Runtime behaviour is marked as not verified.
  • A quoted line must match the source byte for byte.
  • The exploration does not repeat the build-process product names.

5. Research questions

  1. What does a "photoreal" scene without assets require, and how does one site plan keep nine

independent modules in agreement?

  1. How does the startup sequence patch shaders, build modules, collect anchors, bake ambient

volumes, compile programs and capture light?

  1. How does the build(ctx) contract work, and what may a module return and read from other

modules?

  1. How does the patch() system inject GLSL into three.js physical materials, and how does the

program cache stay small?

  1. How does one frame become eight sub-frames, and why does the sampling depend only on the

sub-frame index?

  1. How does the post pipeline use HDR targets, multi-sample anti-aliasing, ambient occlusion,

depth of field, bloom and a mixed tone mapper?

  1. How does the shot system interpolate position, direction, focus, lens and easing, and how do

module-owned overrides replace base keys?

  1. How do the nine world modules build their geometry, and how do they exchange fields and

anchors?

  1. How does the film path move raw pixels from the graphics processing unit (GPU) to ffmpeg

without an intermediate file?

  1. How do the quality tools measure camera motion and temporal flicker, and what does each

metric isolate?

  1. How does the music edit align two musical hits with the cuts at 28 and 42 seconds?
  2. Where does the static reading stop, and which claims depend on a render that this exploration

did not perform?

9. Limits

  • Static reading only. No frame was rendered, no film was viewed and no metric was measured.
  • The film is not in the repository. .gitignore ignores *.mp4 and other media files.
  • The music is not in the repository. tools/fetch-music.mjs downloads it from the network.
  • The reference images are not in the repository. .gitignore ignores ref/.
  • The render output folder out/ is ignored. The checkout holds no render input or render output.
  • No browser was opened. WebGL 2 behaviour, shader compilation, worker behaviour and timings

are not verified.

  • The texture atlases and terrain masks were not drawn or measured.
  • The tools were not run. Their outputs are not checked.
  • The build process is not reproducible here. The page needs a browser and a graphics card.

10. Corrections and limits found during research

The inventory matched: pin 97fd9ec, 121 tracked files, version 1.0.0 and MIT. The following findings qualify the first reading.

  1. The line count is higher than the rounded claim. README.md:15 says "About 31,000 lines

of JavaScript". The src tree holds 31,299 lines and the tools tree holds 612, for 31,911 lines in total. The rounded claim is accurate for src alone.

  1. The triangle count is a runtime value. README.md:15 states 24.4 million triangles. The

value comes from window.__info.tris, which src/main.js:311-315 computes after the world is built. No static file states the count. This exploration did not build the world, so the value is not verified.

  1. "Every shot opens on a macro close-up" is a design intent, not a lens rule.

README.md:17 makes the claim. The first key of each shot has focus distances of 1.05, 0.6, 0.9, 1.35, 1.3, 4.0, 2.8, 1.0, 3.0 and 1.1 metres. Shot 6 opens at 4 metres with a 28 millimetre lens, and shot 9 opens 3 metres from a lotus pad. They are close-ups, but not all are macro.

  1. Not every surface uses a patched physical material. README.md:13 says "every surface is

procedural GLSL injected into three.js's physically based materials". The sky (src/core/sky.js), the light shafts and dust (src/world/interior-fx.js) and the incense smoke (src/world/props-mat.js) use custom ShaderMaterial objects. They do not use patch(). The sky sets fog: false on its material and uses its own atmosphere model. The pond water (src/world/pond-water.js) is a patched physical material, and its shader calls applyAerial directly.

  1. The silent render and the scored film are two products. tools/render.mjs writes a video

stream only. tools/score.mjs joins two music sections and muxes the audio onto a copy of the video. The release film has music, but a fresh render does not.

  1. The film tools assume a 70 second cut. src/shots.js:226 derives the duration from the

shot count. tools/render.mjs:103 uses a literal 70 seconds when --to is absent. tools/score.mjs:34 sets FILM = 70.0. docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:269 states that several tools assume the 70 second cut.

  1. Eight module override files cover nine shots. src/shotkeys/ holds nine files: one index

and eight module files. terrain.js exports an empty object. Seven module files replace nine shot definitions. docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:224 describes the nine owned shots correctly.

  1. The shadow map comment is stale. src/core/lighting.js:29 says "the 4k shadow map is

spent where the camera looks". The default parameter is 4096, but src/main.js:62 passes 8192 at final quality and 2048 at preview quality. README.md:161 and docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:29 give the correct values.

  1. The sub-frame count depends on the mode. README.md:18 says each film frame averages

eight sub-frames. src/main.js:30 sets eight in render mode, four in still mode and one in preview mode. The claim applies to the film only.

  1. The camera is clamped at a cut, but the animation clock is not. In render mode,

src/main.js:379 clamps the camera time to the current shot. setTime() at src/main.js:169-175 receives the unclamped film time plus the shutter offset. The rule "motion blur never spans a cut" therefore applies to the camera path, not to module animation time.

  1. Anchors need the props module. src/main.js:113-123 collects anchors from every module,

then asks props.placeAnchors() to place lanterns and bells. If the query string selects modules without props, the anchors are collected but no lantern or bell is placed.

  1. The music file has a checksum guard. tools/fetch-music.mjs:18 records the SHA-256 of

the file used to time the edit. A different file raises a warning, because the cut points may miss the musical hits.

  1. The build is not an offline film build. npm run build runs vite build for the browser

page. The film has no bundling step and no build output. It runs through the dev server and headless Chrome.

  1. Preview and final quality have different settings. Preview uses a 2048 squared shadow

map, 10 ambient-occlusion samples and no depth of field. Final uses an 8192 squared map, 16 samples, depth of field and a scene-captured environment. The readme gives the same split.
