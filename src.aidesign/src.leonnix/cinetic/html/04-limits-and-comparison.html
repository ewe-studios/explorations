Limits and comparison: what is measured, what is judged, and what the siblings do

Two ledgers

cinetic is an Agent Skill that makes a coding agent build a short film. An Agent Skill is a folder of instructions and tools that a coding agent loads. This document reads the source at commit f4bbfc8 and compares cinetic with taste-skill and tastecode, the other two packages in this exploration series. cinetic keeps two ledgers. The first ledger holds what a script measures. The second holds what a person or a model judges. The evaluation states the split (docs/evaluation.md:11).

The split matters because a pass on one ledger is not a pass on the other. A film can hit every frame rate, loudness target and beat line and still be dull. The review guide defines three severity levels. P0 means priority 0: a fault on a key beat, a broken story or fact, or a broken hard ban (review-loop.md:343). P1 means priority 1: a fault noticeable on a normal viewing. P2 means priority 2: a fault visible only on pause (review-loop.md:344-345). The ship gate is the list of conditions that must hold before delivery (review-loop.md:347). The director checklist names a flat field (a ground with no depth or light) with small type as a P1 even when every gate passes (director.md:74-77).

# docs/evaluation.md:5-11
In short: across six judged rounds, cinetic met more of the written expectations than the baseline every time and won every house-style comparison. The neutral blind judge was split: 7 wins in 23 comparisons over the six rounds, and 2 of 4 in the last, on a snapshot of 1.0.0. Against the films of 0.5.0, the last version whose films were judged, 1.0.0's films won 5 of 8 blind comparisons. A cinetic run costs about three times the time and tokens of the baseline.

## Why evaluate a creative skill

cinetic asks an agent to do much more work than a plain attempt: invent a brand, score the sound, render motion blur and run review rounds. It also rules out a list of common looks. Any of these choices can be wrong. A rule that removes the generated look can also remove something viewers like. And a skill can make every film pass its own checks while people still prefer the plain version.

Part of the question can be measured. Is the file the size, frame rate and length the brief asked for? Is the loudness on target? Does the loop have a seam? Do the hits land on their frames? The rest is judgement, which needs a comparison against a real alternative, made by judges who do not know which film came from which process. The evaluation does both and reports them separately.

The first ledger covers the file size, frame rate, duration, colour tags, stutter, loop seam, loudness, true peak, audio tail and the audio, alpha and still files. An alpha file is a video with a transparent channel (docs/evaluation.md:105). It also covers the beat grid, cue grid, copy rules, safe zone, hue count, layout, sync and frame forensics. The second ledger is the neutral rubric's eight dimensions: idea, motion, type and layout, colour and brand, product truth, sound, finish and brief fit (docs/evaluation.md:106). The director pass adds the hook, picture, composition, transitions and pacing (director.md:33-79).

What the gates measure

Each gate measures a proxy.

grid-check.ts imports the timeline and checks the beat, the acts, the cue grid, the copy, the lone line, the type size and the text hold. It also checks the script shape, the fragments, the concurrent copy, the read-first rule, the reveal rate, the chain gap, the chain beat and the anti-metronome rule. It also checks the cut-on-sound rule, the spine rules, the deal rules, the grounds, the subject run, the scene length and the lockup end (grid-check.ts:4-17). It does not measure whether the scene is worth watching.

forensics.py reads the frames. It computes the mean absolute change between frames and classifies the spikes, stalls, holds, quiet windows, the hook, ghost frames, borders and judder. It also classifies sharpness, banding, smear and loop seam. A spike is a change above 1.0 and 2.5 times the median of its neighbours plus 0.3. The rule is precise, and it does not know what the frame shows (forensics.py:12-31).

look-audit.py measures the look. It groups saturated pixels by hue and fails three hue groups. It fails saturated colour in banned regions. It excludes official marks. It measures the accent share, the second hue, the tinted ink, the negative space, the living ground and the clean finish. It also measures the camera lock, the small subject, the type size and the luminance flip. It also measures the blur policy, the first-frame word, the fade, the arrival front-load and the image develop (look-audit.py:15-24). It measures colour physics and composition. It does not measure whether the colour is right for the brand.

copy-check.py reads the script as a stranger would read it with the sound off. It fails long, vague, slogan-like, claim-heavy or verbless lines. It also fails a missing product name, a missing plain description, a placeholder or a kit sample (copy-check.py:1-16). It measures clarity rules. It cannot measure whether the claim is true beyond the brief.

The audio gates are numeric. score.py masters to -14 loudness units relative to full scale (LUFS) with music, -16 LUFS for a film of eight seconds or less, and -18 LUFS for effects only. The true peak ceiling is -1.5 decibels true peak (dBTP) (score.py:59-60). check-sync.py fails when the decoded audio lags the source by more than 48 samples, which is one millisecond at 48 kilohertz (check-sync.py:7-9). av-audit.py looks for each onset within plus or minus two frames. With stems it requires a 90 percent hit rate; without stems a low rate is a warning (av-audit.py:19-21, av-audit.py:32).

             128, delta 0.03, or a > 1 kHz 1 ms-envelope rise >= 4x); hit rate >= 90%     fail
             With --stems the sfx stem is searched (strict) and stray sfx onsets are listed.

The gates are not independent: they share assumptions. The beat grid assumes that the analysed downbeats are the right cut points. The copy check assumes that the brief is true. The look audit assumes that the declared accent is the brand's accent. A wrong assumption passes every dependent gate.

What judgement covers

A comparator is a judge that sees two films in random order as A and B. The neutral comparator scores the eight dimensions from 1 to 5; the total is 40 points, or 35 for a silent film, because sound is not scored (docs/evaluation.md:106). It is blind: it does not know which film came from cinetic (docs/evaluation.md:110). The house-style comparator uses the same rubric with a guide that restates the hard bans (docs/evaluation.md:107). The previous-version comparator repeats the neutral comparison against the film of 0.5.0 for the same brief (docs/evaluation.md:108). The baseline is the same agent without cinetic, with the official Remotion skills (docs/evaluation.md:99). The evaluation uses four judges. Only the expectation grader knows which film is cinetic's (docs/evaluation.md:105). The other three are blind. The house-style result is a compliance check, not independent evidence that viewers prefer the result (docs/evaluation.md:186).

## Setup

Each round ran every brief with cinetic at the version under test and compared the result against a baseline:
- **with cinetic**: the agent pointed at a snapshot of the skill at the version under test and told to follow its SKILL.md;
- **baseline**: the same agent without cinetic, but with the official Remotion agent skills (`remotion-best-practices` and its companions).

The source also states the judge limit. All the judges are language models, not viewers, and they may share blind spots with the agent that made the films (docs/evaluation.md:185).

What the evaluation shows

The evaluation shows five things.

  1. The films met more written expectations than the baseline in every judged round (docs/evaluation.md:5, docs/evaluation.md:114-122).
  2. The house-style comparator chose the cinetic film in every comparison from round 3 to round 7 (docs/evaluation.md:118-122). Round 1 has no house-style result, so the clean sweep covers five rounds (docs/evaluation.md:124).
  3. The blind neutral comparator was split. cinetic won 7 of 23 comparisons, and 2 of 4 in the last round (docs/evaluation.md:5). The 23 come from the six judged rounds; invalidated round 2 adds none.
  4. In the previous-version comparison, the 1.0.0 films won 5 of 8 blind comparisons against the 0.5.0 films, over four briefs and two rounds (docs/evaluation.md:5).
  5. A cinetic run costs about three times the time and tokens of the baseline (docs/evaluation.md:5).

What the evaluation does not show

One run per brief per round means that the variation between runs is not measured. The baseline films never changed, so their movement is judge noise: the neutral judge scored one unchanged baseline film up to 7 points apart (docs/evaluation.md:184). All judges are language models, not viewers. The house-style judge scores against cinetic's rules. The expectations favour the skill, because they were written alongside it (docs/evaluation.md:184-187).

The evaluation does not show causality. cinetic bundles many changes: the deal, the copy rules, the sound pipeline, the motion blur and the review round. A win does not show which change caused it, and the rounds are small.

The evaluation does not show reproducibility. The film history lives outside the repository, at ~/.cinetic/film-history.json or the path in CINETIC_FILM_HISTORY (film-diff.mjs:49). The licensed track is not committed, because audio/music/ is ignored (README.md:193). A fresh machine cannot reproduce the exact choices.

The evaluation does not show that the gates generalize. The judged results cover four of the seven briefs. No brief covers a user-interface walkthrough, a film longer than 25 seconds or a social cut-down (docs/evaluation.md:191).

The evaluation does not ship its harness, the program that runs the briefs and judges. The repository ships the briefs and expectations only (docs/evaluation.md:197).

Real failure modes in the source

A failure mode is a way the system can fail or mislead while its gates pass. The source also has documentation and release defects.

The deal can weaken its own avoidance. pick removes the avoided values, but falls back to the full list when every value is avoided (deal.ts:48-52). The scene deal also falls back from fresh to free to pool0 when the filters empty the pool (bank.ts:184-188). The film therefore never runs out of choices. It can repeat a recent choice when the pool is small. A repeat weakens the promise that each film differs from the last three. The repeat is best effort, not a guarantee.

# skills/cinetic/assets/templates/launch-simple/src/deal.ts:48-52
const pick = <T extends string>(seed: string, slot: string, from: readonly T[], avoid: readonly string[] = []): T => {
  const free = from.filter((x) => !avoid.includes(x));
  const pool = free.length ? free : from;
  return pool[hash(`${seed}:${slot}`) % pool.length];
};

The history is outside the artifact. A new machine starts with no film history. The first film on that machine has no "last three films" to avoid. The code is reproducible. The history-dependent choices are not.

The registry can go stale. film.json is written only when it does not exist (new-film.mjs:218-223). A folder reused with --force keeps its old devices and old recent list. The scaffold does not merge or refresh them. The agent must notice.

The constant setter is narrow. setConst matches the first const NAME = value; line only (new-film.mjs:249-256). It dies if the line is missing. A source edit that renames the constant or changes its shape breaks the scaffold. The constraint couples the scaffold to the template text.

The render can fall back to an upscaled draft. When the final cannot finish, render.sh --from-draft scales the whole draft up and delivers it (render.sh:25-27). The source prefers a complete draft over a cut final. The delivered file is still a lower-quality master.

The sync gate is weaker without stems. Stems are the separate audio parts before the mix. av-audit.py gates sync only with --stems. With the full mix, a low hit rate is only a warning, because the music hides quiet effects (review-loop.md:211). A film without stems can pass the audit with weak sync.

Declarations can hide a problem. The review guide lets the agent declare cuts, freezes and ignores, so the scripts do not flag designed moments (review-loop.md:172-187). The guide warns: "Do not declare your way to a pass" (review-loop.md:187). The rule depends on the agent's honesty. A declaration is a human override, not a measurement.

The look audit's full run is advisory. The default review uses look-audit.py --look, not the full run. The floors, the minimum values a measure must reach, are advisory (review-loop.md:79-82). The look gate can find a flat film, but the default review may not gate on it.

The engine licence differs. cinetic uses the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) licence, but Remotion does not. It is free for individuals and firms up to three people, and a company licence is needed from four people (README.md:194-195). A company that adopts cinetic adopts that licence too.

The default frame rate is stated in two places. SKILL.md:196 says films render at 30 frames per second (fps). The template ships with FPS = 60, and the scaffold rewrites it from --fps, whose default is 30 (new-film.sh:21-28, new-film.mjs:249-262). The scaffold resolves the conflict. It does not resolve the evaluation's 60 fps rule (docs/evaluation.md:187). The template alone shows 60.

The second-round rule has two versions. The short path says a second round happens only when a gate fails (SKILL.md:176). The review guide says a second round happens when a gate fails or a P0 was fixed (review-loop.md:73). The wider rule is safer. The short rule can leave a fixed P0 unverified.

The version line citation in the specification is off by one. The specification cites .claude-plugin/plugin.json:4 for the plugin version (spec.md:172). The file has "version": "1.5.1" on line 5. The value is right and the line is wrong.

Comparison with taste-skill

taste-skill is an Agent Skill for company websites. Its own description says it makes coding agents build professional company websites at the level of figma.com and x.ai, instead of assembling a generic landing page (taste-skill/README.md:61). It is version tasteskill 2.5.1 (taste-skill/README.md:61, taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:4). It is MIT licensed (taste-skill/README.md:13). It needs Node 22 or later and Chrome, Edge or Chromium; ffmpeg is optional (taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:28).

The two packages share a form. Both are Agent Skills with a contract, references and scripts. Both fight the generated look. Both use a seeded or dealt element to make each result differ. Both have a review step and a gate that can fail.

The gate placement is different. taste-skill arms a Stop hook: a command that the agent host runs each time the agent stops. The hook runs gate.mjs (taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:14-19). The checker opens the page in a headless browser, which is a browser with no visible window, and runs the checks one at a time, each in its own profile. It checks the page at 1440x900 and 390x844, plus consistency, copy lint, kit edits and checker gaming (taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/references/gate.md:32-45). Checker gaming is page code that tests for the test browser, such as navigator.webdriver, and changes its behaviour (taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/references/gate.md:45-46). It counts rounds, with three by default. A waiver is a recorded exception with three fields: the check identifier, the target and a reason of at least 12 characters (taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs:47-53). One waiver covers one finding only. More than five waivers is itself a failure, and a waiver cannot cover a kit edit or checker gaming (taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs:52-53). cinetic has no Stop hook. The agent runs the gates in the eight steps. A Stop hook is stronger, because it runs on every stop, even when the agent forgets. cinetic relies on the contract and the time plan. Nothing outside the agent forces the fast gates (review-loop.md:24-39).

The outputs are different. The taste-skill package produces a website, so it can measure the document object model (DOM), the layout, the tap sizes and the copy. The cinetic package produces timed media, so it measures the frame, the soundtrack and the beat. The film gate measures the delivered artifact at more levels, but it is more expensive and more indirect.

The review artifacts are different. Taste-skill keeps .taste-skill/build.json, .taste-skill/gate.json and waivers. Cinetic keeps film.json, out/qa/*.json, out/cues.json and the film history. Taste-skill's record is per build; cinetic's record is per film and per machine.

The maturity is different. The taste-skill package is a 2.5.1 skill with a browser gate and a clear scope. The cinetic package is a 1.5.1 production system with a larger toolchain: Node.js, Python, ffmpeg, Chromium and earshot. It also buys more ways to fail at install time.

Comparison with tastecode

tastecode is a local desktop workspace for Artificial Intelligence (AI) coding agents, with Design Mode built in (tastecode/README.md:7). It is early beta version 0.1.1 (tastecode/README.md:9). It is Apache 2.0 licensed (tastecode/README.md:94-95). It runs on Windows 10 or 11 x64 or macOS on Apple Silicon (tastecode/README.md:44).

Its architecture is server-first. A local server owns all state and orchestration, and every client is a thin renderer over a typed WebSocket protocol (tastecode/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:11-12). The server holds the orchestration, the adapters, the SQLite event log, the checkpoints, the worktrees and the pseudo-terminal (PTY) (tastecode/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:19). Its Design Mode is a phase pipeline. The phases are Brief, Brand, Page, Asset, Build and Review (tastecode/docs/DESIGN-AGENT.md:55). Five of the six phases write an artifact; Build writes the preview. The artifacts are .taste/brief.json, .taste/brand.json, .taste/page.json, .taste/assets.json and .taste/review.json (tastecode/docs/DESIGN-AGENT.md:83-111). The artifacts are a chain of evidence. A later phase may consume earlier artifacts but never silently rewrite them (tastecode/docs/DESIGN-AGENT.md:252-255).

The two systems solve different problems. The tastecode package owns the runtime, the state and the provider sessions. The cinetic package is a skill that runs inside an agent; the agent owns the work, and the project folder owns the state. The tastecode server has a database and resumable phases. The cinetic project has a file-based project and a film history.

The gate placement is again different. The tastecode server validates the phase artifacts and checks them at phase boundaries. The server restores a changed artifact and rejects a phase result that breaks the contract (tastecode/docs/DESIGN-AGENT.md:444-451). The cinetic skill validates the timeline before render. It validates the frames after render.

The review artifacts are similar in spirit. The tastecode server writes review.json with the latest visual verdict and actionable findings (tastecode/docs/DESIGN-AGENT.md:263). The cinetic skill writes the director's JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) with issues, frames, priorities and fixes (director.md:98-110). It also holds 13 checklist answers and a score from 0 to 10 (director.md:98-110). Both turn a review into data. Neither can prove that the review is right.

The maturity is different. The tastecode package is an early beta that expects bugs and rough edges (tastecode/README.md:9-10). The cinetic package is a 1.5.1 skill; its six judged rounds tested 0.1.0, 0.3.0, 0.4.0, 0.5.0 and two 1.0.0 snapshots. The snapshots are not the release: round 7 tested a development build of 1.0.0, and no judged round covers the released 1.0.0 or any later version (docs/evaluation.md:124, docs/evaluation.md:189). The tastecode product is larger; cinetic is smaller, with a deeper media pipeline.

The taste-skill package is the simplest to enforce, because the browser is the artifact. The tastecode package is the most structured, because the server owns the phases. cinetic is deepest in media, with the widest toolchain and most runtime dependencies.

What a reader should trust

A reader should trust the source's measurements more than its claims. The gates are real scripts. The evaluation is a report about a small set of runs. The neutral result is split, the house-style result is a compliance check, and the cost is about three times the baseline.

A reader should not trust the numbers as a general quality score. The evaluation covers four briefs, one run per brief per round, and a judged version that is not the released version. The judges are language models, and the expectations were written alongside the skill (docs/evaluation.md:187). A win is evidence of a better fit to these expectations, not evidence that a real audience prefers the film.

A reader should trust the failure modes more than the success stories. The deal can fall back and repeat a recent choice. The history is outside the artifact. The registry can go stale. The sync gate is weaker without stems. The declarations can hide a problem. The render can deliver an upscaled draft. The full look audit is advisory. The source names most of these ways to pass and still be wrong.

Aha: the three packages put the gate at different layers, and the layer decides what can be measured. The taste-skill package gates the browser, so it can measure layout and interaction. The tastecode package gates the server phases, so it can validate artifacts and state. The cinetic package gates the timeline and the frame, so it can measure timing, colour, motion and sound. No layer can measure whether the work is worth making. That question stays with the director, the neutral judge and the user.
