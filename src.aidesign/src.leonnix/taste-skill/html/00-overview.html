Overview: the taste-skill product

Taste Skill is an Agent Skill for web design. It builds company pages that show a real product or place. The source is a Git checkout at pin f6b7657. The version is 2.5.1 (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:4). The skill folder holds 1,631 tracked files, and the repository holds 1,663 (spec.md).

The product is not only a text prompt. It is a contract, a kit, and a set of measuring scripts. The contract states what a good page must contain. The kit supplies the parts. The scripts measure the built page in a real browser. A Stop hook makes the measurement a condition for finishing.

This document explains the product, the contract, the nine house rules, the kit, and the actors. The reader needs no prior knowledge of the source.

1. The problem the product answers

A general model can write HyperText Markup Language (HTML) and Cascading Style Sheets (CSS) for a page. The result often looks generic. The page repeats a small set of habits: a centred hero, a row of three feature cards, a stock photograph, and a slogan. The model can also claim that the result is good, because no external check contradicts the claim.

Taste Skill answers both problems. It fixes the visible habits with a kit of page parts and a set of house rules. It fixes the unverified claim with scripts that fail a page on measured faults. The finish gate then blocks the agent until the page passes or the agent records the open faults.

The skill also limits its own scope. It serves company websites, product pages, service pages, and place pages. The bar is stated as figma.com and x.ai (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:26). The bar is a reference for restraint and craft, not a source to copy.

3. The nine house rules

The nine rules "win over everything else in this skill" (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:32). They are short, absolute, and measurable in part. The first two rules protect the visual surface.

# skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:30-36
## House rules

These nine win over everything else in this skill.

1. **No caption or description under any image, render or visual.** Licence credits go in one
   footer line.
2. **Every visual renders, never an empty ground,** at 1440, 1024, 768 and 390.

Rule 1 removes explanatory text under pictures. A licence credit goes in one footer line. Rule 2 requires every visual to render at four widths: 1440, 1024, 768, and 390 pixels (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:36). The checker tests this requirement with the assets, empty-visual, hidden-visual, and fold-card checks.

Rules 3 to 5 control layout and motion.

# skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:37-45
3. **One section per screen:** the hero alone on the first screen, every section exactly one or
   two screens tall at 1440x900, no second section heading peeking in. The one exception is a
   live `photo-chapters` section, three or four screens, set by the moment.
4. **Equal left and right content edges.**
5. **Hover micro-animations:** a linked card lifts 2px, a photo zooms 1.02-1.03 inside its frame,
   buttons step on hover and press on click, underlines draw, card loops cycle in 2.5-4 s, repeat
   and restart on hover. The motion layer does it; never strip or override it. No tilt, carousels
   or timed tabs. Every hero enters once on load (`data-enter`), and every page carries one dealt
   moment, two on a long page (the gate holds both).

Rule 3 makes one screen the unit of section height. A screenshot at scroll position k times the viewport height must show exactly one section (skills/taste-skill/references/layout-v4.md section 2). Rule 4 keeps the left and right content edges equal. Rule 5 names the allowed motion: small lifts, zooms, presses, and underlines. It forbids tilt, carousels, and timed tabs. It also requires a hero entrance and one or two dealt moments on every page. A dealt moment is a finished section that a weighted dealer chose from the kit, not a new animation by the agent.

Rules 6 to 9 protect the content and the ethics of the page.

# skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:46-56
6. **More and better real photographs where they fit;** nicer backgrounds; better heroes, layouts
   and fonts.
7. **Real full-colour icons** (a known product's real icon, never a drawn one); small diagram
   visuals in cards (a hub with branches; one input, a main processor, three parallel lanes;
   pulses running along the lines).
8. **The big award-site scenes and creative moments are part of the skill:** every page deals its
   moments (photo- and type-led off tech), but shader effects and scenes only where the trade has a
   technical subject (tech, AI, developer tools, gaming, fintech, hardware), never on a local cafe,
   bakery, guesthouse or shop: those get photographs.
9. **Addresses:** street and district only (no street given: the district), no house number, postcode or coordinates; no register
   or VAT numbers; no phone in a real format; contacts on `.example`.

Rule 6 asks for real photographs over drawings or renders. Rule 7 asks for real product icons and small diagram visuals. Rule 8 limits shader effects and large scenes to technical trades. A local cafe or guesthouse gets photographs instead. Rule 9 limits addresses to a street and a district. It forbids house numbers, postcodes, coordinates, register numbers, value added tax (VAT) numbers, and real phone formats. Contact addresses use .example.

The rules do not stand alone. Each rule has at least one checker identifier. Rule 1 maps to caption. Rule 2 maps to assets, empty-visual, and hidden-visual. Rule 3 maps to one-screen, section-gaps, and fold-card. Rule 4 maps to col-edge, right-edge, and split-align. Rule 5 maps to hover, hover-move, and reduced-motion. Rule 6 maps to photo-set, photo-upscale, and photo-weight for the photograph part. The layout and type part lands in the layout check and the system checks. Rule 7 maps to assets and photo-set. Rule 8 maps to effects-fit. Rule 9 maps to copy-fact and fact-agree.

Aha: The nine rules work as a contract because the checker gives each one a name. A rule that has no check is still text. A rule with a check becomes a failure that the gate can report.

4. The six kinds of business

The skill first classifies the brief. The classification decides the page type, the allowed assets, the heroes, the moments, and the length of the page. The six kinds are software, gaming, tech, product, service, and place (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:66-73).

# skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:66-73
| Brief | page type | site-kind | kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| App, SaaS, AI, developer tool, fintech or banking app, a studio selling its own software | software | software | software |
| A game, a studio launching its game | software | gaming | gaming |
| Hardware or a tech device (a drone, a robot arm, a keyboard) | product | tech | tech |
| Any other physical product or brand (a chair, a bike, coffee beans sold online) | product | other | product |
| People are hired: clinic, law firm, agency, creative studio, trade | service | other | service |
| People come to it: cafe, bar, bakery, restaurant, guesthouse, small hotel, shop, florist, bike shop, gym, local studio | place | other | place |

The page declares its kind in two meta elements: and . A place page adds (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:60-64). The dealers read the last column. The hero dealer reads the page type. The checker reads the metas.

flowchart TD Brief["Read the brief"] --> Door{"Do people come<br/>to the door?"} Door -->|"Yes"| Place["place"] Door -->|"No"| Hired{"Are people<br/>hired?"} Hired -->|"Yes"| Service["service"] Hired -->|"No"| Game{"Is it a game?"} Game -->|"Yes"| Gaming["gaming<br/>site-kind gaming"] Game -->|"No"| Tech{"Is the subject<br/>technical?"} Tech -->|"Yes, hardware"| TechKind["tech<br/>page type product"] Tech -->|"Yes, software"| Software["software"] Tech -->|"No"| Product["product"]

The distinction between tech and product matters. tech means a technical subject. Only tech pages may use shader effects, large scenes, loops, counts, dark grounds, and dark heroes (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:78-81). A chair, a bike, or a coffee brand is product. It gets photographs, flat colour, type, real icons, a hero entrance, a dealt moment, and the hover layer.

The distinction between place and product also matters. A ceramics studio that teaches classes is a place. A bakery with a web shop is a place, and the shop becomes the goods section. The goods section is the part of the page that lists what the shop sells. A business is a product only when nothing happens at the door (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:75-76).

Aha: The kind is not a label for the report. It is a permission system. It decides which assets the page may load and which checks apply.

5. The kit

The kit is the part that makes the output look designed. It lives in skills/taste-skill/assets/. A build copies the kit into the project (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:160-161). The kit is not a blank framework. It holds finished page parts that the agent assembles and rewrites for the client.

The palettes and the type pairings live in library/. They are not part of the asset kit. The start script reads them, and a build does not copy them into the page as kit parts.

The hero set has 11 models: centred, collage, editorial, film, photo, place, product, split, statement, triptych, and word (assets/layout/hero-*.html). The agent copies one hero and rewrites every word and picture. It never copies the hero's nav, footer, other sections, or credit line (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:233-235).

The dealer chooses a hero by page type and available material. The command takes --has flags for photographs, user interface, film, object, and setting (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:203). The choice is weighted, and the dealer keeps a history. Two pages of the same kind do not get the same hero.

The reason goes on the plan (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:200-228). A history file prevents a repeat of the current goods.

The moments set holds 18 items, such as photo-chapters, word-highlight, pinned-chapters, picture-develops, letters-assemble, and cursor-spotlight (assets/moments/). A moment is a finished section with its own markup and script. The agent mounts it in the page. It does not write a new animation.

The photograph library holds 413 entries and 16 trade groups (assets/photos/photos.json). Each entry has a licence, a source, a credit line, and slots such as hero, use, place, or portrait (skills/taste-skill/references/photos.md). The photograph script can list, pick, search, fetch, and print credits. It can also record a stand-in when the client supplies no photograph.

6. The actors

The system has eight actors. Each one has a clear job.

flowchart LR Client["Client brief and material"] --> Agent["Design agent<br/>the model"] Agent --> Skill["SKILL.md and 22 references"] Agent --> Dealers["Dealers: hero, palette, type, moment"] Dealers --> Kit["Kit: starter, heroes, UI, moments"] Kit --> Page["Built page"] Agent --> Lint["copy-lint.mjs"] Lint --> Page Page --> Checker["consistency.mjs"] Page --> Layout["layout check"] Page --> Perf["perf.mjs"] Checker --> Gate["gate.mjs"] Layout --> Gate Perf --> Report["The build report"] Gate --> Hook["Stop hook<br/>Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity"] Gate --> State[".taste-skill state files"] Hook --> Agent

The client supplies the brief and the real material: the name, the logo, the product, the photographs, the prices, the address, and the hours. The skill forbids invented reviews, quotes, customers, and figures (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:372). A claim that the brief does not support must be marked as assumed.

The agent is the model that reads the skill. It classifies the brief, writes the copy, plans the screens, deals the parts, builds the page, runs the checks, reads the screenshots, repairs the faults, and writes the report.

The dealers are four command line programs. assets/layout/deal.mjs deals the hero. scripts/start.mjs deals the palette and the type. scripts/moments.mjs deals the moments. scripts/effects.mjs deals the effect on technical pages. Every dealer keeps a history, so a series of pages varies.

The kit is the set of finished parts. It includes the starter document and the system. It also includes the heroes, the user interface fragments, and the diagrams. The remaining parts are the moments, the effects, the scenes, the objects, and the components. The kit also holds the photographs. The palettes and the type are library data, not kit parts.

The checker is scripts/consistency.mjs. It opens the page in headless Chrome and measures layout, text, navigation, buttons, motion, visuals, copy, wiring, and system rules. It reports each identifier as PASS, WARN, FAIL, or not checked (skills/taste-skill/scripts/consistency.mjs). Two other scripts measure layout stress and performance. The gate runs the checker and the layout check. It does not run the performance script, which the agent runs by hand.

The gate is scripts/gate.mjs. It merges the checker results with static checks, applies waivers, counts rounds, and writes the verdict (skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs). The gate has eight verdicts: PASS, FAIL, OPEN, QUICK, SETUP, ERROR, DONE, and OFF (skills/taste-skill/references/gate.md:113-123). QUICK is a look between rounds and never closes a build. DONE follows the user's stop words.

The hook is the Stop event handler. In Claude Code and Codex, it can return a block decision. The agent then continues the work instead of finishing. In Antigravity, the hook returns a continue decision instead (skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs:111).

The state files are small documents in .taste-skill/. They record the build marker (the build.json file), the gate result, the waivers, the block history, the build completion, the unarmed note, the missing-hook note, and the checker crops. The gate.json file holds a history array. The dealers also keep separate history files for the palettes, heroes, effects, grounds, and scenes. The next round reads the gate state, so the gate is not stateless (skills/taste-skill/references/gate.md:113-150).

7. What the product promises

The product promises five results:

  1. A page that names what is sold and shows the real thing on every screen.
  2. A page with a fixed system of space, type, colour, and edges.
  3. A page with real photographs and real product icons.
  4. A page with a measured pass on a fixed list of checks.
  5. A report that states the gate line, the waivers, the open faults, and the missing client

material (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:291-296).

The product also promises a limit on effort. The gate allows three rounds by default, with a maximum of ten (skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs:653). It stops the agent from an endless repair loop. It also allows the user to stop the build with gate.mjs --done "" (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:280-282). The command needs the user's words, with 12 characters or more. It does not run in hook mode (skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs:668-670).

8. What the product does not promise

The skill does not promise that the page is beautiful. The checker measures named properties: overflow, overlap, contrast, spacing, tap size, image weight, and copy patterns. It does not measure taste in the sense of a good composition. A page can pass every check and still be dull. The separate performance script reports speed and weight. The gate does not run it.

The skill does not promise that the client material is true. The checker can read a fact and compare its repeats. It cannot know whether the fact is correct. The skill marks assumed facts and asks the agent to report them (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:294-296).

The skill does not promise that the hook works in every environment. It depends on the hook support of Claude Code, Codex, or Antigravity. The hook command writes a missing-hook note when it cannot find gate.mjs (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:18). This package did not test the hook behaviour of those products.

The skill does not promise a good result on a page outside its scope. It targets company, product, service, and place pages. It is not a tool for a large web application, a dashboard, or a shop with a checkout flow.

9. The never list

SKILL.md ends with a short list of forbidden results. The list is a useful summary of the product's ethics and its taste.

# skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:367-372
- A card, row or tile that fades in on scroll; a fade-in on every section; parallax on text;
  scroll-jacking; random blinking.
- Stock photographs passed off as the client's product, people or place (a stand-in is listed in
  the report as one, never shown with another maker's name or logo); a product page that never
  shows the product.
- A drawn look-alike of a real logo; invented reviews, quotes, customers or figures.

The list continues with violet or lavender in the page's own design, copying a reference site, and editing the kit to clear a check (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:373-377). The violet rule is unusual. It prevents a common model palette. It also prevents a page from looking like a default output of the model.

The last item is the most important for this package.

# skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:376-377
- Deleting or editing the kit's own files (`assets/`, `scripts/`) to clear a check: a check that
  flags a kit file is reported, and the page is checked again after any change.

The agent must not edit or delete the kit's own files to clear a check. A fault in a kit file is reported, and the page is checked again after a change (skills/taste-skill/SKILL.md:377). The gate also detects kit edits with the kit-edited static check (skills/taste-skill/scripts/gate.mjs:365).

11. Facts not verified in this package

The package is a static reading of the source. No script was run. No browser was started. No page was built. Runtime behaviour is therefore not verified.

The specific unverified points are:

  • The actual visual result of a GATE: PASS page.
  • The actual false-positive rate of the checker.
  • The actual hook behaviour in Claude Code, Codex, and Antigravity.
  • The actual speed of a check on a large page.
  • The actual quality of the 413 photographs and their licences at the time of a build.

The source makes claims about each point. This package reports the claims and marks them as claims. Where the source gives a number, this package repeats the number and cites the line. Where the source gives no number, this package writes that the value is not defined.
