08 -- Contributing: submit, correct, or remove a work

Contribution is simple: open an issue. There is no pull-request flow, no build to run locally, and no form. To submit a work, a contributor opens an issue with a link to the creator's original post on X. To correct or remove a work, the creator opens an issue or sends a direct message (DM) on X. This document explains both paths and the one rule that decides what counts as a prompt.

The guide is CONTRIBUTING.md. It is written in both English and Chinese in one file. It is hand-written and never overwritten by the sync workflow.

Submitting a work

The submit section, from CONTRIBUTING.md lines 5-9:

Open an issue with the link to the creator's **original post on X**. A work is listed when:

- the post is by the creator and has a native video attached;
- the post has at least 5,000 views;
- the post states the work was made with Claude Opus 5.5.

Four conditions must hold. The post is the creator's own post. The post has a native video. The post has at least 5,000 views. The post states the work was made with Claude Opus 5.5.

Note a small wording gap. This section names only Claude Opus 5.5. The README and the inclusion_rule field in cases.json name all four models: Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5, and Fable 5.5. The contribution guide is narrower than the actual gate. In practice a submission is checked against the four-model rule described in 01-inclusion-criteria.md.

Supplying a prompt

The guide then tells the contributor where the prompt can live, from CONTRIBUTING.md line 11:

If the creator published the prompt, point to where: the post itself, their own reply, a screenshot in their reply, or a link they shared. Prompts are kept verbatim. Descriptions of the work, tool lists and paraphrases are not prompts.

The contributor does not paste the prompt into the issue as a rewrite. They point to the source. The four allowed locations are the post body, the creator's own reply, a screenshot in that reply, or a link the creator shared. The prompt is kept verbatim. A description, a tool list, or a paraphrase is not a prompt. This is the same rule documented in 05-prompts.md.

The issue-only flow

%%{init: {"flowchart": {"htmlLabels": false}}}%% flowchart TB PERSON["person finds a work"] --> ISSUE["opens an issue"] ISSUE --> CURATOR["curator verifies the post and the prompt source"] CURATOR --> UP["upstream jasonzhu.ai data is updated"] UP --> SYNC["daily sync pushes the new record here"]

The contributor never edits cases.json directly. The issue is reviewed upstream. The daily sync then brings the new record into this repository. The sync pipeline is documented in 07-maintenance.md.

Corrections and removal

The second section of the guide, from CONTRIBUTING.md line 18:

Creators can ask to correct attribution or remove a work at any time: open an issue or DM [@GoSailGlobal](https://x.com/GoSailGlobal) on X. All works belong to their creators; inclusion grants no license.

Two ideas matter. First, the creator owns the work. The catalog does not claim a license by listing it. Second, the creator can act at any time: open an issue or DM @GoSailGlobal on X. The same text appears at the end of the README.

The Aha Moment

Aha: Contribution is a pointer, not a paste. The contributor submits a link, and the prompt rule says to point at the source, never to reproduce or improve the instruction. This keeps the catalog's quality out of the contributor's hands and in the evidence: the curator only records what the creator actually wrote and where. The contributor's job is to find the work and the source; the catalog's job is to preserve both verbatim.
