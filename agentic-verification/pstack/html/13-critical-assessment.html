Critical assessment: what pstack gets right, and its costs

pstack is a serious, opinionated toolkit by an engineer who has shipped at scale. Its best ideas are portable and worth copying; its costs are complexity, a heavy model dependency, and a high barrier to the formal-verification tooling.

The verdict in one line

The philosophy — go deep, verify against the real artifact, encode lessons in structure — is strong and coherent. The implementation is real (it ships a working TLA+ charting tool, not prose about rigor). The price is that pstack is a whole method with its own vocabulary, its own model routing, and its own toolchain, and it expects you to adopt all three.

Strengths, ranked

  1. Confidence as a first-class type. epistemics.md turns epistemic honesty into five tiers with per-tier phrasing rules — "because" is banned in inference, "obviously" is banned outright, and "an honest we-don't-know is one of the most valuable outputs." This is the single most transferable idea in the plugin, and it costs nothing to adopt.
  1. Structure beats prose, and it is enforced. encode-lessons-in-structure and correct's ladder (architecture → types → lint → test → docs last) are applied to the plugin itself: skills ship scripts (statechart, check-plan.mjs, worktree-audit.sh, log.sh) that enforce what the prose describes.
  1. A real formal-verification tool, not advice about it. The statechart tool drives TLC, colors violating states, verifies the drawn chart against the model (regionsLie), and escapes Mermaid input adversarially. Tests cannot reach races and retries; this tool can.
  1. The cross-judge is a different family. arena forces its judge to come from a model family other than the parent's, structurally preventing a model from rubber-stamping its own biases.
  1. Playbook steps become the todo list verbatim. The mode skill copies playbook steps directly into the todo list, so execution follows named, reproducible steps rather than a paraphrase.
  1. Off-by-default skills. disable-model-invocation: true everywhere except setup-pstack means the plugin never burns tokens auto-firing skills a turn does not need.

Weaknesses

  1. It is a whole method, not a tool. 58 skills, 23 playbooks, a 2,374-line pull-request watcher, a formal-verification toolchain, and its own guide. A user who wants one idea (say, unslop) has to wade through the entire vocabulary. The entry cost is high.
  1. The defaults name specific model slugs. grok-4.7-xhigh-fast and claude-opus-5-5-xhigh are hardcoded as defaults. setup-pstack verifies slugs against the available set, but the routing is opinionated and vendor-specific; a rename or a pricing change breaks the defaults.
  1. The formal-verification path has a steep barrier. To use the statechart tool a user writes TLA+ and a view module. Most engineers will not. The tool is excellent for the few who can; it is out of reach for the many who cannot.
  1. Cursor-first. Custom modes, automations, and ~/.cursor paths are native; Claude Code support is a 67-line adapter. The plugin works in Claude Code, but it is a guest, not a citizen.

Risks

  1. Fearless parallelism is expensive. The whole method fans out subagents, cross-judges, and reviewers. A user who adopts the philosophy without the budget will see large token bills before they see the quality gain.
  1. Model routing ages. Hardcoded model IDs are a bet on the current frontier. When the frontier moves, the defaults lag — and the plugin's advice (which model to trust for judgment) is exactly the thing that most needs updating.
  1. "Never block on the human" can over-run. The autonomy split proceeds on reversible work without asking. The boundary between reversible and irreversible is a judgment call, and an agent that misjudges it acts without confirmation.
  1. Cargo-culting the method. The playbooks are prescriptive and well-reasoned, but a user who follows them without understanding the principles gets the ceremony without the judgment. The tooling does not replace the engineer; it assumes one.

Aha moments

Aha: the plugin's best ideas are free to copy. Confidence tiers, the fix ladder, and structure-over-prose are pure method — they cost nothing to adopt and improve any workflow, with or without pstack.

Aha: rigor is implemented, not described. The reason pstack is credible is not the principles; it is that the principles are backed by scripts that fail loudly. The statechart tool, check-plan.mjs, and log.sh are the proof of the prose.
